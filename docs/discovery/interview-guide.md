# The interview guide

> **SIMULATED.** The questions below are asked of a persona — a role-play written for this repository
> — and the answers they produce are staged. Nothing here records a real interview.

The arc of a session, in the order the conversation is taken. This is the floor of it, not the
ceiling: when a persona names something these questions do not reach, follow it and note the detour in
the record. [question-bank.md](question-bank.md) is the lookup the later sections dip into;
[process-walkthrough.md](process-walkthrough.md) is what sections 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 are filled into.

## How to ask

- **Ask for the last real case, not the process.** "Walk me through the claim you closed yesterday"
  produces the work; "how does the process work" produces the ideal. The ideal is what the persona
  was taught, and the gap between it and the last case is the finding.
- **Open first, close to pin.** Let the persona describe; go back afterwards for the value, the date,
  the system's name, the threshold.
- **Use the persona's own words**, in Slovak where the work is Slovak, and record the term. A word the
  persona uses and this repository does not is a term the specification is missing.
- **Ask where the rule is written down.** The answer is usually "nowhere" — and that is a rule that
  has to leave the persona's head and enter the specification.
- **Ask what happens on the bad day**: the long queue, the unreadable scan, the policy number that
  returns nothing. A description of the tool that works says nothing about the workaround that keeps
  it running.
- **When the persona says "it depends", ask what it depends on.** That is a branch, and branches are
  what the walkthrough template has fields for.
- **Time the waits.** How long does it wait for the police record, for the customer, for the queue,
  for the colleague who is on leave? The wait is where the process actually lives.
- **Ask what the persona would not let a machine do**, and take the answer at face value rather than
  negotiating it. That answer is a requirement about authority.

## 1. The role

*What it is for: the frame. It produces the persona's scope, so that later answers stay inside it.*

- Describe your job to somebody who has never worked in motor claims.
- What is on your screen when you start the day?
- How many claims are yours right now, and how many arrive on a normal day?
- What is the thing you are measured on that you would rather not be?
- What do you hand to somebody else in the course of a claim, and what do they hand back to you?

## 2. One claim, from arrival to closure

*What it is for: the walkthrough. It produces the filled process template.*

- Take the last claim you closed and walk me through it from the moment it arrived.
- What arrived, and who touched it before you?
- What did you have to type rather than copy?
- Where did it wait, and why?
- What made you stop and look something up?
- What did you have to check with somebody else before you could move?
- What would have gone wrong if you had gone faster?

## 3. The documents

*What it is for: what the system will actually receive, in the shapes it actually arrives in.*

- What documents arrive with a motor claim here, and which of them do you trust?
- Which one do you read first, and what do you look for in it?
- What does a bad one look like — a scan, a photograph, a fax, a rewrite in a letter?
- When a value is unreadable, what do you do: ask again, guess from the rest, or drop it?
- Which number on which document is the one you would not proceed without?
- How would I know from a submission that it is incomplete before I have read it all?

## 4. The decision

*What it is for: how the claim is actually decided, and which part of it is a rule at all.*

- What are you deciding when you open a claim: covered or not, how much, or something else?
- Where do you look up whether something is covered?
- How do you know which edition of the conditions applies?
- What is the threshold above which you ask somebody else, and who?
- What makes you send a claim to the fraud unit?
- What have you seen a colleague do with a claim that you would not do, and why not?

## 5. The system and the register

*What it is for: the systems of record, and where the work leaks out of them.*

- Which systems do you have open at once while working a claim?
- What do you type into two of them?
- What do you keep in your own notes, and why is it not in the system?
- Where do you look to know where a claim actually is, as opposed to where it says it is?
- What does the register do when a submission arrives that it has seen before?
- What goes wrong when two people work the same claim?

## 6. The exceptions and the workarounds

*What it is for: the part of the work nobody writes down. It produces the walkthrough's exception and
workaround sections.*

- Work [question-bank.md](question-bank.md)'s exception section straight down, for this role. "That
  is not mine" is a valid answer and is recorded as one.
- Then its workaround section, the same way.
- Every answer that arrives as a story rather than a rule is followed with "where is that written
  down?"

## 7. The close

*What it is for: the material the critique is built from, and the end of the session.*

- Show the thing now — the specification, the prototype, the run — and react to it part by part.
- What does it get right, in your words?
- What does it get wrong that would cost you time tomorrow?
- What does it get wrong that would cost the insured money, or the office a dispute?
- What would you refuse to do because the system told you to?
- What do you want it to tell you that it does not?
- If this were the only thing that ever changed about your day, what would still be broken?
