# The question bank

> **SIMULATED.** These questions are put to a persona, a role-play written for this repository, and
> the answers are staged. Nothing here records a real interview.

The lookup the session's later steps dip into: the exceptions the happy path hides and the workarounds
nobody writes down. It is consulted, not read straight through, and it is a **floor** rather than a
closed list — a session that only answers these has found only what was already anticipated.

Ask each question of the role that owns it. A question outside a persona's role is answered "that is
not mine" and recorded that way; [PERSONAS.md](PERSONAS.md) has the boundary for each role and which
of the four opening exceptions each meets at which moment.

## The happy path, briefly

The questions that establish the baseline, so that an exception has something to be an exception to.
The rest of [interview-guide.md](interview-guide.md) works these out at length.

- What arrives, and what makes a submission complete?
- What does the handler do first, and what does it do last?
- Which value does everything else get checked against?
- What does a claim that never comes back to anybody look like?

## Exceptions — the four the bank opens with

|Exception|Question|
|---|---|
|A mandatory document is missing|Which documents are mandatory, for which peril, and who says so? What is registered while one is missing? How long is the claim allowed to wait for the police record before somebody decides without it? What does the customer get told?|
|A plate (*EČV*) or VIN does not match the policy|Which is checked against which — plate against policy, VIN against the estimate, the estimate against the notification? What is a plausible mismatch and what is a refusal? Where does a mismatch send the claim?|
|A field cannot be read|Which fields does the work actually stop on when they cannot be read? Is a second document used to fill the first, or is the customer asked again? How is a value that was guessed marked, and does the guess survive into the decision?|
|The same claim was submitted twice|What makes two submissions the same claim — the policy number, the incident date, the plate, the customer's name? Which one is worked and what happens to the other? What does a near-duplicate look like — a resubmission with one more page attached?|

## Exceptions — dates and cover

- What happens when the incident date falls outside the period the policy was in force, and who
  decides that it does?
- The notification names an incident date and the police record names another: which is the date the
  claim is adjudicated against, and what makes the choice?
- Which edition of the poistné podmienky applies, and how is that worked out today?
- A claim arrives for damage that happened before an endorsement took effect: what is checked, and by
  whom?
- How is a claim treated that arrived months after the incident, close to whatever limit applies to
  notification?

## Exceptions — the money

- What ceiling means the handler can decide alone, and what happens just above it?
- The estimate says one figure and the invoice says another: which is paid, and what is written down
  for the difference?
- What is deducted before payment, and where does the deductible figure come from?
- A repair was already paid by the customer and the invoices are for reimbursement: what changes in
  the handling?
- What is a claim amount that makes no sense at all — a total loss figure on a scratch — and what
  happens when an amount is simply wrong?

## Exceptions — the parties and the vehicles

- Who can submit a claim — the insured, the driver, the owner, the garage, the other insurer?
- The driver is not the insured and is not on the policy: what is asked, and what does it change?
- A liability (*PZP*) claim arrives where the other side's insurer is also involved: what is handled
  here and what is handed over?
- The vehicle in the documents is not the vehicle the policy names: who resolves that?
- A theft with no police record, and a theft where the vehicle turns up: what is different?
- The accident happened abroad: which documents are demanded that a domestic claim would not need?

## Exceptions — the documents themselves

- A document arrives in a format nothing can read, or as a photograph of a screen: what is requested
  back?
- The same document arrives twice under two names, or one file holds two documents: what is attached
  to what?
- A document belongs to another claim already in the register: what happens when it is attached here?
- What makes a document worthless in the handler's eyes — no date, no signature, no stamp, no amount?
- A page is missing from the middle of a bundled scan: how is that caught today, and what catches it?

## Workarounds

- Where do you keep the list the system does not have — the private spreadsheet, the saved e-mail, the
  notebook, the memory of the colleague who has been here longest?
- What do you type into two systems because they do not talk, and which one do you trust when they
  disagree?
- What do you skip when the queue is long, and what brings you back to it?
- What do you do that is formally wrong because it is the only way the claim moves?
- Which rule do you know exists in writing and follow as you were taught instead?
- Who do you phone, and what do you ask them that no system records?
- What did the person who trained you tell you that no document says?

## Tacit rules and authority

- What threshold do you carry in your head that is not written down anywhere?
- When do you escalate without being asked, and when do you decide alone because asking would take too
  long?
- Who can override whose decision, and who is never allowed to?
- What may a handler never do, whatever the claim looks like?
- What has to be true before anyone here would let a claim pass with no human looking at it?

## Evidence and trust

- Which document do you believe when two of them disagree, and is that the same answer for every
  handler?
- What would make you distrust a value a machine read out of a document, even if it looked right?
- What would you have to see, attached to an answer, before you would act on it without checking the
  original?
- What does an answer have to tell you about its own uncertainty for it to be usable?

## What the persona is measured on

- What number would your manager ask about if a claim went wrong?
- What is a claim that is closed correctly but counts against you anyway?
- What would you want to be true about this claim six months from now, if somebody asked you why you
  decided as you did?

## What a persona would not let a machine do

- Which decision would you refuse to have made for you, even if the machine were right more often than
  you?
- What would you want to be told, rather than asked?
- Which single mistake by the system would make you stop using it?
